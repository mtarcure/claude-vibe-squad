## Deliverable 1: Voiceover script

Third-person past tense, close on Isaac. Two verbatim source quotes, marked with quotation marks. Word count stated below the script and verified mechanically (method noted).

> The television was gone. A wall clock hung in its place. The mortgage envelope stayed unopened.
>
> Hailey said they'd find another way. He said he was sorry, kissed her, and walked into the November dark.
>
> Hummingbird Consulting paid for one thing: memory. Three days of his mind, chemically segmented, sealed away. He had never signed so many papers.
>
> The pod closed like a cocoon. Cold climbed his veins. He held on to Hailey — her hair, her smile, her eyes. What color were they? Something dark. Whose smile was that? What was her name?
>
> Nothing.
>
> White. He woke reading the world like data: a replaced ceiling tile, three months old; footsteps — male, one-eighty, excited.
>
> The client was HydroChem. A cheap filter for cities that couldn't afford clean water. The design was locked, all but the binder. "That's your sandbox—go play."
>
> He solved it fast — then kept looking. Buried in the scaffold: an engineered ester linkage, built to break. Eighteen months, then a hidden enzyme reservoir would flood every tap downstream. HydroChem owned the only cure. A manufactured monopoly on drinkable water.
>
> Any fix would fail QA — and the only man who knew would forget.
>
> Only the binder was his. So he made it bleed. A slow-eroding skin, shedding a harmless tracer HydroChem itself had patented — tripping the city's fluorescence monitors in thirty days. Seventeen months early. A signal with a return address.
>
> He finished ahead of schedule.
>
> Thursday felt like Monday.
>
> Cereal. Isaac filled a glass from the tap.
>
> The morning news: executives in handcuffs, HydroChem on the wall. A harmless chemical, Hailey said, gave them away in time.
>
> "Wow. What a crazy world."

At ~145 wpm with dramatic pauses this reads at ~113–124s.
